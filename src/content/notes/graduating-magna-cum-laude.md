Finishing college with **Magna Cum Laude** honors while studying Information Systems at DFCAMCLP wasn't something that just happened by memorizing lecture slides. Honestly, it came down to a lot of late nights, black coffee, and stubborn persistence over four years.

Most of my classmates treated coursework as separate from the real world. You turn in an assignment, get a grade, and forget about the database schema the next morning. Early on, I realized I couldn't learn that way if I actually wanted to build things that worked for real businesses.

So instead of waiting until graduation to look for work, I started taking on real projects right in the middle of our semesters.

### Real Code vs. Homework

There's a massive difference between an app that passes a teacher's test rubric and software someone actually relies on to run their business:

- **Enterprise & Freelance Builds:** When I was building BizMaker HRIS ERP and Bubble Hideout POS, a bug wasn't just a deduction of a few points—it meant an employee's shift hours didn't calculate correctly or a cashier couldn't balance their register. That kind of pressure teaches you clean database schemas, transactions, and role permissions faster than any textbook ever could.
- **48-Hour Hackathons:** Heading to the DICT eGovPH National Hackathon in BGC with our team was a reality check. You have two days, live government APIs to hook into, and zero sleep. It showed me how to strip away the fluff and focus purely on what solves the problem right in front of you.
- **Cybersecurity & Blue Teaming:** Grinding through the Cisco Junior Cybersecurity Analyst curriculum opened my eyes to how fragile web apps really are once you look at packets traveling over the wire in Wireshark.

### What the Latin Honor Means to Me

People often look at Latin honors and think it's all about obsessing over a GPA. For me, graduating Magna Cum Laude was just proof that you don't have to choose between building real software and doing well in school.

The theory I learned in class helped me design safer, cleaner databases for clients. And dealing with real, messy production bugs made exams a lot less intimidating because I had already lived through the failure states.

It was four intense, chaotic years, but walking out of college with both hands-on engineering chops and highest academic standing made every single sleepless night worth it.
